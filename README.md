# LG-consumer-behavior-digital-marketing

📊 Impact of Digital Marketing Strategies on Consumer Purchase Behaviour: A Study of LG Electronics

📌 Project Overview

Type: Primary + Secondary Market Research Report.
Domain: Digital Marketing | Consumer Behaviour | Electronics Industry.
Subject: BBA — 2nd Year / 4th Semester.
Institution: PSIT College of Higher Education, Kanpur (CSJMU Affiliated).
Industry Focus: LG Electronics — South Korean MNC with presence in 100+ countries.
Core Question: Do LG's digital marketing strategies (social media, SEO, influencer campaigns, e-commerce) actually convert consumer awareness into purchase decisions?

🎯 Business / Research Objective
Problem Statement

LG deploys multi-channel digital marketing (Instagram, YouTube, Flipkart ads, influencer partnerships) — yet not every digital touchpoint converts to a sale.
Gap identified: A consumer may like an ad but still buy Samsung; may ignore social media but buy after reading one Flipkart review.
Challenge: Pinpointing which digital channel has the highest purchase-conversion impact for LG's product categories (TVs, refrigerators, ACs, washing machines).

Research Objectives
Primary:

✅ Analyse LG's digital marketing strategy across channels.
✅ Examine consumer purchase decisions for LG products.
✅ Measure the actual impact of digital marketing on buying behaviour.

Secondary:

🔹 Identify top digital channels influencing purchase (social media vs. search vs. e-commerce).
🔹 Assess social media's role in brand recall and preference.
🔹 Evaluate the influence of online reviews on final decisions.

Hypotheses Tested

There is significant consumer preference for LG within the electronics segment.
Perceived product quality is the primary driver of LG purchase decisions.
Family members play a critical role in the final buying decision.

🔬 Methodology
Research Design

Descriptive research design adopted to systematically describe consumer awareness, preferences, and purchase behaviour.
Chosen because the study aims to report what exists, not establish cause-effect relationships.

Data Type

Primary Data — collected directly from 101 consumers via structured questionnaire.
Secondary Data — sourced from research papers, academic journals, LG Global Newsroom, Statista, and DataReportal.

Primary Research Tool

Structured questionnaire built and distributed via Google Forms.
Included both close-ended questions (MCQ, rating scales, preference-based) for quantifiable data and open-ended questions for deeper consumer opinions.

Sample Size & Sampling

101 respondents selected — adequate for a descriptive, academic-level study within limited time and resources.
Convenience + Purposive Sampling — targeted digitally active individuals most exposed to online marketing.

Target Respondents

Students (56%), Young Professionals (20%), Business owners (19%), Salaried individuals (6%).
Age group focus: 18–25 years (71%) — highest digital platform engagement.

Distribution Channels

WhatsApp groups, college email networks, and direct digital outreach.
Enabled quick reach and mobile-friendly completion.

📂 Key Areas Covered

Digital Marketing Channels Studied: Social Media (Instagram, YouTube, Facebook), Search Engine Ads (SEO/SEM), Influencer Marketing, E-commerce platforms (Amazon, Flipkart), Email Marketing, Official Website.

Consumer Decision Factors: Product quality, Brand image, Price, After-sales service, Online reviews, Word-of-mouth.

LG Product Categories: Smart TVs, Refrigerators, Washing Machines, Air Conditioners, Microwave Ovens.

Company Analysis: SWOT Analysis, Competitor mapping (Samsung, Sony, Whirlpool, Haier), Organisational structure, Vision-Mission-Values.

Consumer Behaviour Theory: Buying process stages — Awareness → Interest → Evaluation → Purchase → Satisfaction.

📈 Data Analysis & Findings
Survey Data Highlights (n = 101).
Brand Selection Factor.

65.3% respondents ranked Product Quality as the #1 factor when choosing a brand.
Only 7.9% cited price — consumers prioritise durability and reliability over cost.
Brand image ranked 2nd at 21.8%; after-sales service was least influential.

Refrigerator Brand Ownership

Samsung leads at 37.6% — strongest brand recall and digital visibility.
LG holds strong 2nd position at 26.7% — competitive but gap remains.
Whirlpool at 21.8%, Godrej at 9.9%, Others at 4%.

Purchase Decision Influencer

54.5% decisions are family-driven — household appliances are a collective buying decision.
28.7% decide independently — based on personal research, online reviews, and comparisons.
Friends & relatives influence only 11.9% — peer impact is relatively low.

Customer Satisfaction with LG

77.2% respondents are satisfied with LG products — strong brand trust and product-market fit.
Only 7.9% dissatisfied — indicating isolated product/service experience issues.
14.9% said "Don't Know" — signals engagement gaps post-purchase.

Age-wise User Distribution

71% LG users fall in the 18–25 age bracket — LG's strongest consumer base is youth.
26–34 age group accounts for 23%.
35–50 age group is the smallest at 8% — limited middle-aged penetration.

Occupational Distribution

Students dominate at 56% — majority of respondents are young, digitally active consumers.
Professionals at 20%, Business owners at 19%, Salaried at 6%.

Key Findings

📦 Quality > Price — 65.3% prioritise durability & reliability; only 7.9% cited price as the top factor → consumers are willing to pay a premium for dependable products.
🥈 LG holds strong #2 position — 26.7% refrigerator ownership behind Samsung (37.6%); competitive but gap remains.
👨‍👩‍👧 Family drives the cart — 54.5% of purchase decisions are family-influenced; appliances are a collective household investment.
🎯 Youth is LG's core market — 71% of LG users fall in the 18–25 bracket, directly correlating with digital-first behaviour.
✅ High satisfaction rate — 77.2% customers satisfied, indicating strong product-market fit; 14.9% "Don't Know" signals engagement gaps.
📱 Digital marketing acts as the discovery engine — online ads, YouTube demos, and influencer content initiate purchase journeys; final conversion still often happens in showrooms.

💡 Recommendations

Deepen influencer + demo-driven campaigns — Product demonstrations (live/digital) build confidence; LG's "Affectionate Intelligence" 2025 campaign is a step in the right direction.

Target family decision-makers directly — Since 54.5% decisions are family-led, campaign messaging should address the collective household, not just the individual buyer.

Double down on the 18–25 segment — Reels, YouTube Shorts, and campus ambassador programs can increase LG's youth stickiness vs. Samsung.

Leverage authorized showrooms + exchange offers — Consumers prefer buying from branded outlets; exchange deals (old appliance for new LG product at discount) were flagged as strong purchase triggers.

Improve post-purchase digital engagement — Personalized follow-up emails, warranty reminders, and content marketing post-sale can convert satisfied customers into brand advocates.

✅ Conclusion

Digital marketing is no longer just a promotional tool for LG — it is the primary consumer behaviour driver in the electronics segment.
Quality + Brand Trust + Family Input form the core purchase decision triangle for LG's product categories.
LG's 77.2% satisfaction rate and strong #2 position in refrigerators confirm solid brand equity — the gap vs. Samsung lies in digital storytelling, not product quality.
The study confirms: digital marketing initiates the journey, but product reliability and in-store experience close the sale.
Key takeaway: LG must combine its manufacturing excellence with emotionally resonant, youth-first digital campaigns to maintain and grow market share.
